The Challenge

For The Kids is a Utah-based nonprofit serving over 350 children every week. Despite its meaningful impact, the organization faced significant challenges in donor communication and revenue diversification — limiting its ability to grow and sustain programming.

As part of the GOFF Strategic Leadership Institute, I was selected to work directly with the organization and develop a real, actionable marketing strategy.

Implementation Roadmap

The strategy was built around a phased rollout — from launching social media and an intern program in month one, to a full campaign expansion over two-plus years.

What I Delivered

  • 65-page strategic marketing plan with a year-long implementation roadmap
  • Complete social media strategy for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts — projected to generate 10–25 new monthly donors
  • 15+ customizable email templates and an annual communication calendar for donor retention
  • "Christmas in July" fundraising campaign with a 4-week rollout plan and corporate matching component
  • Merchandise strategy and vendor recommendations for a sustainable revenue stream
  • Internship program structure to increase organizational capacity by 10 hours weekly

Campaign: Christmas in July

A 4-week summer fundraising campaign designed to create urgency and engagement during an off-peak donation period.

4-week campaign rollout timeline

Donor gift strategy by tier

Merchandise Strategy

Branded merchandise designed to turn donors into ambassadors — the "Carry Our Mission With You" tote line was developed as a low-cost, high-visibility revenue stream.

Key Takeaways

Real client, real stakes
Working with an actual nonprofit meant every recommendation needed to be practical and implementable with limited resources.
Data-driven storytelling
Projected outcomes needed to be grounded in research — benchmarking social media performance and fundraising data across comparable nonprofits.
Multi-channel thinking
Effective nonprofit marketing requires coordinating email, social, events, and merchandise into one cohesive strategy.
Stakeholder communication
Presenting a 65-page plan to organizational leadership required clear, confident communication and the ability to defend strategic recommendations.

Bloom Nutrition:
Integrated Advertising
Campaign

A full advertising campaign for a wellness brand targeting women 18–35 — positioning Bloom as the simple, trustworthy daily nutrition solution in a crowded supplement market.

BrandBloom Nutrition
TargetWomen 18–35
TypeIntegrated Advertising Campaign

The Challenge

Bloom Nutrition operates in an oversaturated wellness market where consumers — particularly young women — are overwhelmed by competing brands, confusing health claims, and decision fatigue. The challenge was to position Bloom as the clear, approachable, trustworthy choice without losing the brand's energy and personality.

Campaign Strategy & Mantra

The campaign was grounded in a sharp insight: decision fatigue is real, and wellness shouldn't feel like another job. The "Adulting is Better This Way" platform positioned Bloom as the one decision you don't have to overthink.

Print Ad Series: "Adulting is Better This Way"

Three print executions featuring real adulting stress moments — getting fired, a car tow, a rent increase — paired with the message that at least your greens are handled.

Activation Merchandise

A branded merchandise line designed for the "Shake It With Bloom" experiential activation — turning event attendees into walking brand ambassadors.

What I Delivered

  • Complete campaign strategy using the "Adulting is Better This Way" brand platform
  • 30-second TV commercial concept featuring a color transformation visual storytelling technique
  • Three-part print advertisement series with scenario-based relatable messaging
  • Experiential marketing plan: "Shake It With Bloom" high-energy public workout activation
  • Post-activation social media content strategy including behind-the-scenes and customer interview content
  • Branded merchandise line for brand ambassadors

Key Takeaways

Brand voice & tone
Developing a consistent voice that stretched from a TV spot to a social caption required deep understanding of the target audience's mindset.
Integrated thinking
Every channel — TV, print, experiential, social — needed to feel like one cohesive campaign, not isolated executions.
Insight-led creative
The strongest creative ideas start with a real human truth. Decision fatigue wasn't just a hook — it was the strategic foundation the whole campaign was built on.
Experiential to digital
Designing the "Shake It With Bloom" activation with social virality in mind meant thinking about the event and the content it would generate simultaneously.

Strategic Analysis · Case Study

Campus Market Entry:
A Case Study in
Brand Repositioning

A market entry and brand repositioning strategy for a local food business transitioning from a declining location to the University of Utah campus — targeting undergrad students with a shareable, affordable experience.

Target MarketUndergrad Students, Ages 18–23
FocusMarket Entry & Brand Strategy
TypeStrategic Case Study

The Challenge

The business was operating at a location that was underperforming and needed a new direction. The opportunity: reposition the brand and enter the University of Utah campus market — a high-volume, price-sensitive environment with strong social sharing behavior and consistent foot traffic.

The core challenge was identifying the right target segment, simplifying the brand message, and building a go-to-market plan that would resonate with a college audience.

What I Delivered

  • Market segmentation analysis identifying three distinct customer groups with behavioral and demographic profiles
  • Target market recommendation: undergraduate students ages 18–23, based on volume potential and spending patterns
  • Brand repositioning strategy focused on eliminating confusion and centering shareability and affordability
  • Streamlined product strategy emphasizing core offerings and group dining occasions
  • "Finals Week Special" pricing strategy to capitalize on peak demand periods
  • "Grand Opening Week" promotional campaign with sampling, student organization partnerships, and late-night delivery
  • Location and timing recommendations based on campus foot traffic analysis

Key Takeaways

Market segmentation
Identifying the right target segment — not just the largest one — was the most important strategic decision in the entire project.
Local market insight
Understanding campus culture, foot traffic patterns, and student spending behavior informed every recommendation in the strategy.
Brand clarity
Simplifying the brand's identity — removing what confused customers and doubling down on what resonated — was central to the repositioning.
Go-to-market planning
A strong launch strategy with a clear timeline, promotional hooks, and community partnerships is what turns a repositioning into real traction.
